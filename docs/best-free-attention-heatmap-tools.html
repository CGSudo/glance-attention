Best free attention heatmap tools for your next review

For a free attention heatmap, start with Glance if you want a local design workflow. UNISAL and DeepGaze IIE provide research code; pysaliency helps evaluate predictions; the manual squint test checks composition without software. These options solve different jobs. Choose by the job you need done

“Best” depends on whether you need a design review, research implementation, metric evaluation or team service. This is not a measured performance ranking. Free code does not automatically grant rights to model weights or datasets.

What each free route gives you

Glance is the design-workflow route: keep files local, inspect a prediction and follow the model citation. A browser check makes the first image easy to try; desktop inference can run offline after setup. Check the download page before relying on a particular release. Browser runtime and model caching have separate availability constraints.

DeepGaze IIE and UNISAL are research routes. Expect to prepare inputs, manage dependencies and interpret model outputs. The DeepGaze IIE paper and UNISAL paper explain their methods. They do not establish that every conversion or export reproduces the published benchmark.

Use pysaliency when your question is how to evaluate a model. Use squinting when your question is whether the composition reads as a few clear shapes. Neither belongs in a feature checklist as though it were a hosted design review suite.

Read the map before acting

Predicted attention is not observed gaze, comprehension or conversion. Keep model, crop and region boundaries fixed when comparing variants. Read the accuracy guide, then follow the landing-page workflow to choose one question for a real user test.
