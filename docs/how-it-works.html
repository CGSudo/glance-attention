How Glance guesses where you look

Your eyes sample a scene. Glance learns from those patterns.

  1. Your eye sees a small sharp patch.

    Imagine a tiny spotlight. The fovea, near the center of your retina, gives roughly 2° of sharp vision. The rest helps you notice where to look next.

  2. Your eyes jump, then pause.

    The jumps are saccades, roughly 3–4 per second. Pauses called fixations often last 200–300 ms. People look differently when they have different goals.

  3. Researchers teach a model with gaze records.

    Recorded fixations show patterns across images and viewers. A model learns which image features tend to attract those fixations.

  4. Glance guesses a map for your image.

    An installed model runs locally and assigns relative attention across pixels. Darker teal in this illustration means a stronger guess. A map describes a pattern, not one person’s measured gaze.

Questions before you start

Is the gaze order a recording?

No. Without a scanpath model, Glance uses peak picking with inhibition of return and a center prior. This is an approximation, not a recorded sequence.

Can I use a saliency map to prove usability?

No. A saliency map estimates attention. Usability testing is needed to check whether people complete their tasks.
