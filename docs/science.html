Read the score before trusting it

Glance estimates early visual attention. Each score answers a narrower question than “does this design work?”

What goes into a heatmap

Let pᵢ be normalized, nonnegative predicted fixation density at pixel i, with Σpᵢ = 1. An area of interest (AOI) is a region you draw. Attention share describes predicted map mass, not a percentage of viewers.

Metrics for comparing predictions with people

Let S be a predicted saliency map, G a human fixation density and F the set of recorded fixation locations.

MetricFormulaMeaning and limit
AUC∫₀¹ TPR(FPR) dFPRHow well map scores separate fixated locations from negatives; 0.5 is chance under the chosen protocol. Negative sampling and center bias matter.
CCcov(S,G) / [σ(S) σ(G)]Pearson correlation of map values, −1 to 1; larger is more similar. Undefined for constant maps.
NSSmean(i ∈ F) [(Sᵢ − μ(S))/σ(S)]Average standardized map value at human fixations; higher is better. It is not a percentage. Undefined when σ(S) = 0.

Commercial “92–96% accuracy” figures may normalize a metric against the human ceiling. For example, 83% / 90% ≈ 92%. That ratio does not mean “correct 92% of the time.” Request the raw metric, denominator, dataset and validation procedure for each claim.

Where prediction stops

  • Early free viewing, roughly the first 3 seconds, differs from reading or goal-directed tasks.
  • Training data may not represent your audience, image type or viewing context.
  • Map concentration and model agreement do not measure understanding, preference, memory or emotion.
  • Model export, resizing and preprocessing can change results. Export parity remains to be verified.
  • Use real participants for decisions requiring actual behavior, accessibility or conversion evidence.

Questions before you start

Is Glance 94% accurate?

No such Glance result is established. Reference benchmark values describe a named research model and protocol, not this application.

Does higher clarity mean a better design?

No. Clarity measures concentration in the predicted map. A very concentrated map can overlook useful information.
